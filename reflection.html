My Week 4 Reflection Summary

My week 4 reflection summary where I learn about semantic HTML, review what I already knew about CSS and learn and improve on concepts I was not aware of. I'm also reflecting on the difference between <article> and <section> HTML elements.

Moving on from Divs

One of my biggest takeaways this week was finally connecting the dots between the traditional <div> approach and modern HTML5 semantic elements. Years ago, when I first started building web pages, I relied almost entirely on <div> tags and classes to contain and style content. I remember seeing tags like <header>, <footer>, and <article> later on, but I never really understood the difference. I just assumed they were two ways of doing the exact same thing.

Learning about HTML5's improvements with accessibility changed my approach. I now understand that a <div> is just an invisible, meaningless container that provides no direction to screen readers. By replacing them with semantic elements like <header>, <main>, <section>, and<article>, I am actually giving screen readers a clear direction that is useful. The real "aha" moment was realizing I can still apply CSS directly to these semantic tags just like I used to with div classes. This allows me to style the page how I want while keeping the code accessible and foundationally correct.

Article and Section: When to use which?

While working through the assignment, I initially struggled with when to use an <article> versus a <section>. After looking at some of the readings and extra resources, it finally clicked: an <article> is something that can be understood independently and is reusable, like a plug-and-play module you could drop into three different blog posts. A <section>, on the other hand, relies on the other text that it's with and wouldn't make sense on its own. My mental go-to is now: articles are for reusable content, and sections are not.

This distinction is actually a massive realization for my day job. We have so much overlapping content. Even in this week's conference process guide, I realized the "Staging Folder" phase isn't actually specific to conferences. If I tweaked the wording a bit, it could be a standalone <article> that I also plug into our virtual webinar guides. I stuck with <section> tags for this specific assignment so I wouldn't have to rewrite the text, but learning to identify reusable content elements is going to be a major milestone for how I build training materials going forward.
